Your shared inbox is costing you more than it looks.

A 14-day pilot that classifies, routes and pre-drafts inbound email — with everything sensitive left to a human.

The cost, concretely

For a 12-person firm taking ~80 emails a day into info@: about 375 hours a year spent triaging, forwarding and chasing. At CHF 60/hr blended, that is roughly CHF 22,500 annually of coordination — plus the cost nobody itemises:

  • Missed leads. A quote request that sits 36 hours has usually lost to whoever replied the same afternoon.
  • SLA breaches. Friday 16:00 "seen but not actioned" until Monday.
  • Duplicate work. Two people draft replies to the same thread.

Add GDPR/nFADP exposure from unlogged handling of customer data, and the case stops being soft.

The offer: we start narrow on purpose. One inbox category — quote requests, or appointment changes — for 14 days. One owner. One visible handover. One metric: messages left without an owner at end of day.

What runs

  • Classifies inbound mail: sales, support, billing, recruitment, press, internal, spam.
  • Extracts company, phone and deadlines so the reply doesn't ask for what you already have.
  • Routes with an SLA — P1 items surface immediately, not at the next inbox check.
  • Pre-drafts an acknowledgement. Nothing is sent automatically.

What never gets automated

Legal notices, complaints, billing disputes and security incidents are detected and held for a human, always, before any routing decision is made. That gate runs over the whole message, so sensitive mail can't slip through disguised as a sales enquiry. This is fail-closed by design: it is better to route a harmless email to a person than to auto-reply to a complaint.
